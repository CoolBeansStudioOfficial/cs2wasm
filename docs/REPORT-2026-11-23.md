## What landed overnight (all on main)

Each branch was checked by its own session on the homelab (full suite in both modes, stress, and the self-host
check), and by me on the merge with the affected tests locally.

1. **The heap corruption fix and the collections' move** (`Rt.MoveItems` / `MoveItemsRotated`). An array of a
   cycle-capable type was freed 32 bytes into its block, which broke the self-hosted compiler on large programs.
   Test 240 covers it.
2. **The free path:** a free is about twice as cheap where the class is known. The span and ref keep now works
   through any number of frames; this fixed `First`, `Last` and `Any` reading freed memory.
3. **Field loads by identity:** churn went from 1.58x to 1.33x.
4. **The tree's node path and the round-10 build steps:** the tree median went from 1.6x to 1.3x, and the rebuild
   frame dropped below NativeAOT's.
5. **Churn round 5:** a list's setter is inlined into loops, and a value stored then read back is reused. Churn went
   from 1.35x to about 1.2x.
6. **Known differences from .NET, two rounds:**
   - Fixed: finalizers of derived classes, `ConditionalWeakTable`, weak references into a dying group, 2-D arrays of
     cycle-capable classes, spans through function pointers, a finalizer that grows its own dying group (test 244),
     a member referring to itself (test 247), and a place passed by reference.
   - The suite now has no known gap.
7. **The self-hosted compiler's speed:**
   - Library documentation comments are passed over as plain comments.
   - A library that Roslyn reports only for a rejected extension candidate is left out until needed. This mostly
     benefits programs that use LINQ.
   - `host/snapshot.mjs` (`CS2WASM_SNAPSHOT=<file>`, off unless set) lets a build start from an earlier build's
     memory. Builds from a snapshot are byte-identical, and a stale snapshot is refused: another compiler build,
     another environment, or a changed or added reference.
8. **Threads:**
   - A static escape analysis made precise in seven places.
   - List loops and getters borrow for objects no other thread can reach.
   - Statics laid out by cache lines.
   - The async-iterator trap under load (test 087) is fixed.
9. **Tools:** the self-host check now also compiles larger programs and times the self-hosted compiler. It was
   added after two bugs passed the whole suite and only broke the self-hosted compiler.

## Open

- **Tests 127, 131, 187 under heavy load:** they differ in about one run in a hundred.
  - The fix exists on `claude/load-flakes-1`, which is not merged: identical in 1,000 of 1,000 runs each.
  - With it, the self-hosted compiler is 87% slower on test 088 (171 s against 92 s) and 14% to 22% slower on mid-sized
    programs. The extra time is in the checks made when objects are released (107 s against 4 s); why is not found.
  - The trace is in `docs/AUDIT.md` A3.5, "Test 088 through the self-hosted compiler".
- **Rare hangs with threads, present on the base compiler too:**
  - `tools/thread-notes-hang.cs` hangs about 1 loaded run in 100: an unsynchronised read and replace of a finalizable
    class's array elements.
  - `tools/thread-board-hang.cs` hangs about 1 run in 6 and has not been looked into.
  - The suite's tests 275 and 277 lock their shared objects, so the suite does not hit either.
- **The tree's plain parts are off by default:** with them on, finalizer-revived objects and weak handles stay alive
  (tests 248, 265, 266). Off costs 2% to 5% of the tree's p99.9. The cause is not found.
- **Smaller .NET differences still listed in `docs/AUDIT.md` A3.5:**
  - a table value that holds its key through a field
  - `ReRegisterForFinalize` called twice
  - reflection over compiler-made classes
  - finalizer revival with threads
  - spans in the core library's own code
  - held-item compositions
  - a root that is a member of its group
- **Speed still above NativeAOT's:**
  - Churn's median, 1.2x: the measured floors are the counted store and the NaN test, which stays by the owner's
    ruling.
  - The tree's median, 1.3x: cycle-group bookkeeping.
  - Programs with threads, 1.7x to 9x: cycle groups with threads, and types shared by type.
  - The self-hosted compiler from a cold start on small programs, 1.8x on hello world: parsing and binding. On large programs it is already faster (088: 76 s against 95 s).
- **Not verified on main's final commit `440bb82` itself:** its homelab self-host check and suite were queued at
  07:47 and had not finished when this was written; results will be in `docs/FINISH.md`. The commit before it,
  `f549fb0`, passed the suite 258 of 258 in both modes; its self-host check had not finished either. The merges
  after `f549fb0` (threads, self-hosted speed) were each checked on their own branch merged with an earlier main,
  and on this merge by the affected tests locally.
